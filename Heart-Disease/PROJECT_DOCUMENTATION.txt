CLASS IMBALANCE ANALYSIS & STRATEGY EVALUATION
-----------------------------------------------
Class Breakdown:
  Negative (No HD): 318,988 (91.86%)
  Positive (HD)   : 28,258 (8.14%)
  Imbalance Ratio : 11.29 to 1

Key Findings from Empirical Comparison:
1. Baseline Unweighted Model:
   - High nominal accuracy, but catastrophic Recall (~11-13%). Misses ~88% of respondents with heart disease! Unacceptable in health screening.
2. Cost-Sensitive (class_weight='balanced'):
   - Soars Recall to ~78-80% while preserving high discriminative ability (ROC-AUC ~0.83).
   - Operates directly inside the objective function without manufacturing synthetic survey records or inflating memory.
3. Random Oversampling (ROS):
   - Similar recall (~79%), but duplicates records and inflates training overhead.
4. SMOTE:
   - Synthetic interpolation creates artificial fractional values across discrete survey categories, yielding no tangible gain over cost-sensitive weighting (ROC-AUC ~0.83, Recall ~78%).

Selected Strategy for Downstream ML Models:
- Cost-Sensitive Weighting (`class_weight='balanced'`) as default for tree models and linear classifiers.
- Preserves survey integrity, requires zero memory expansion, and directly maximizes clinical sensitivity (Recall).

2. COMPREHENSIVE VIVA VOCE & TECHNICAL DEFENSE GUIDE
-----------------------------------------------------
Use this section to prepare for academic viva, project evaluations, and oral defenses:

Q1: What is the core problem and prediction objective of this project?
A1: The objective is to build a supervised binary classification system that predicts reported heart disease history (myocardial infarction or coronary heart disease/angina) from demographic, clinical, and lifestyle survey indicators using the 2021 CDC BRFSS dataset. It is a population-level risk assessment tool, NOT a direct clinical diagnostic device or acute ischemia detector.

Q2: Why did you use the CDC BRFSS dataset rather than clinical ICU datasets (like Cleveland)?
A2: Clinical datasets like Cleveland contain only ~300 patient records from tertiary medical centers, suffering from selection bias, high variance, and small sample sizes. The CDC BRFSS 2021 provides 438,693 nationwide survey responses with comprehensive self-reported risk indicators, enabling true population-scale screening with strong statistical power.

Q3: How did you define and construct the target variable?
A3: The target variable is `_MICHD`, a CDC-derived computed variable. In the raw survey:
    - Code 1 = Yes (history of heart attack or angina) -> Mapped to 1 (Positive)
    - Code 2 = No (neither condition reported) -> Mapped to 0 (Negative)
    - Codes 7 (Refused), 9 (Don't know), and blank (Missing) were excluded from supervised training (4,635 rows, 1.06%).
    Target prevalence is 8.14% (35,323 cases out of 434,058 valid rows).

Q4: What is data leakage and how did you prevent it?
A4: Data leakage occurs when information outside the training dataset is used to create or tune the model. We prevented it at three critical junctures:
    1. Target Leakage: Raw variables `CVDINFR4` and `CVDCRHD4` (used by the CDC to compute `_MICHD`) were strictly dropped from predictor matrices.
    2. Split-Time Leakage: Train-test splitting (80/20 stratified) occurred BEFORE fitting any preprocessing transformers.
    3. Transformation Leakage: Missing-value imputers (median/mode) and feature scalers (StandardScaler) were fitted strictly on `X_train`, and only applied (`transform()`) to `X_test`.

Q5: Why is Accuracy an inappropriate evaluation metric for this project?
A5: The dataset is severely imbalanced (91.86% Negative vs. 8.14% Positive, ratio ~11.3:1). A naive Dummy classifier predicting "No Heart Disease" for everyone achieves 91.86% Accuracy, but has 0.0% Recall (it misses every single heart disease case). In a healthcare setting, False Negatives (failing to identify a patient with heart disease) have devastating real-world consequences. We therefore prioritize Recall (Sensitivity), ROC-AUC, and PR-AUC.

Q6: How did you handle the 11.3:1 class imbalance?
A6: We conducted an empirical 3-fold cross-validation experiment comparing:
    - Baseline Unweighted: Achieved 10.92% Recall (catastrophic).
    - Cost-Sensitive (`class_weight='balanced'`): Achieved 80.15% Recall and 0.8519 ROC-AUC.
    - Random Oversampling: 80.13% Recall.
    - SMOTE: 78.98% Recall.
    We selected Cost-Sensitive Learning because it penalizes false negatives proportionally ($w_1 \approx 6.14$, $w_0 \approx 0.54$) directly inside the loss function without creating artificial synthetic coordinates in discrete survey feature spaces or inflating memory requirements.

Q7: What features proved to be the most important predictors?
A7: Consistently across Random Forest, XGBoost, and Logistic Regression:
    1. Age Cohort (`_AGEG5YR`): Odds of disease increase ~1.34x per 5-year step; prevalence climbs from 0.7% (18-24) to 22.8% (80+).
    2. Cumulative Risk Count (`RISK_FACTOR_COUNT`): Strict dose-response gradient (1.1% prevalence at 0 risks to 38.4% at 6 risks).
    3. Self-Rated Health (`GENHLTH_SCORE`): Monotonic increase from 1.8% (Excellent) to 28.4% (Poor).
    4. Prior Stroke (`PRIOR_STROKE`): Odds Ratio = 2.45 (Relative Risk = 4.3x).
    5. High Blood Pressure (`HIGH_BP`): Odds Ratio = 2.31.
    6. Male Sex (`SEX_MALE`): Odds Ratio = 2.04 (10.4% male prevalence vs. 6.2% female).

Q8: Why was XGBoost selected as the final Champion Model over Logistic Regression and Random Forest?
A8: While Logistic Regression provided strong baseline linear calibration (Recall = 80.14%, ROC-AUC = 0.8501), XGBoost demonstrated superior non-linear discriminatory capacity, achieving the highest test-set Recall (82.79%), the highest ROC-AUC (0.8543), and the highest PR-AUC (0.3464). Its built-in $L_1$ and $L_2$ regularization guards against overfitting on survey noise, and its inference pipeline processes test instances in milliseconds.

Q9: How do you interpret the Logistic Regression Odds Ratios?
A9: The Odds Ratio (OR) is calculated as $OR = e^{\beta_j}$.
    - An OR > 1 indicates increased risk. For example, $OR = 2.04$ for Male Sex means that holding all other variables constant, males have 104% higher odds of having a history of heart disease than females.
    - An OR < 1 indicates a protective factor.

Q10: What are the project's limitations?
A10: 1. Self-reported survey data: Subject to recall bias and social desirability bias.
     2. Cross-sectional nature: BRFSS is observational and cross-sectional, establishing statistical association, NOT longitudinal causality.
     3. Population vs. Clinical: Does not replace laboratory lipid panels, coronary angiography, or troponin assays.
